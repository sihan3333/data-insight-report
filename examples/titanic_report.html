Key findings

Survival was strongly stratified by sex and class, not chance: women survived at 74% versus 19% for men, and 1st-class passengers survived at 63% versus 24% in 3rd class — both far larger gaps than any of the numeric correlations alone suggest (fare only correlates at r=0.26 with survival; the real driver is who got priority at the lifeboats, which fare and class only partially capture).

Age is missing for 177 of 891 passengers (20%) — too much to ignore and not obviously random (likely concentrated among lower classes or unaccompanied passengers, which would bias any age-based analysis if the missing rows are simply dropped). Cabin is missing for 687 (77%) — at that rate the column mostly tells you who booked an unusually well-recorded ticket, not who did or didn't have a cabin, so drawing conclusions from cabin data specifically would be misleading.

The Fare distribution is heavily right-skewed with a long tail of expensive tickets (partly multiple family members sharing one fare) — worth noting since a plain mean fare would be misleading without checking the histogram first.
